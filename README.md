1. Introduction
Passwords remain one of the most common forms of authentication, yet they are often the weakest link in security.

Week 3 focused on password security and cracking techniques. The aim was to understand how attackers recover weak credentials and why defenders must enforce strong password policies. I worked with two sets of tools: the industry-standard John the Ripper (JTR) and the custom Networkwalks password recovery utilities. Both gave me practical insight into password cracking and reinforced the importance of strong authentication.

4. Analysis
Attack Perspective: Password cracking tools allow attackers to recover weak credentials, gain unauthorized access, and escalate privileges.
Defense Perspective: Organizations must enforce strong password policies, implement multi-factor authentication, and monitor for brute-force attempts.
Key Insight: Password cracking is practical, fast, and effective against weak passwords — not just theoretical.
5. Personal Conclusion
This week gave me hands-on experience with password cracking. I now appreciate that passwords are only as strong as the effort put into creating them. As a defender, I must:

Promote strong, complex passwords.
Encourage regular password audits.
Push for multi-factor authentication.
Raise awareness about the risks of weak credentials.
By practicing these modules, I've strengthened my ability to think like an attacker while applying defensive strategies to protect systems.
